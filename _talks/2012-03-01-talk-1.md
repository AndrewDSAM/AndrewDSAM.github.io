Course Completion Work presented as a partial requirement for the degree of Licentiate in Physics.

ABSTRACT:
This work investigates the historical evolution of the concept of planetary orbits and their
physical foundation. Starting from the conceptions of Antiquity, which regarded celestial
motion as circular and perfect, it analyzes Ptolemy's geocentric model, Copernicus'
heliocentrism, and Kepler's rupture with the introduction of elliptical orbits. It then discusses
how Newton, with the Law of Universal Gravitation and the laws of motion, provided the
dynamic basis to understand orbits as conic sections, determined by energy and angular
momentum conditions. Modern advances are also addressed, such as the relativistic
correction of Mercury's orbit and the use of orbits in space technology. Finally, the study
proposes didactic strategies for teaching orbits in the classroom, using historical,
experimental, and computational resources.
